Prologue — Friday, May 2, 2026

History's Future

Introduction — Technological Singularity

Not in the sci‑fi sense — no glowing orbs, no robot overlords, no dramatic takeover. The singularity is here, but visible only in the quiet, invisible acceleration of intelligence, computation, and data that underlies the substrate of life on Earth. It is evident in the widening gap between what humans can understand and what our machines can generate. It's here in the way that the very tools we made can now learn faster than we do.

We are living through the most consequential transformation in the history of our species, and most people are too busy scrolling on their devices to notice. The 2026 AI Index from Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence, AI's annual report card, cuts through a lot of noise.

If you're following AI avidly as I do, you're probably getting whipped like cream. People have all sorts of definitions; AI is huge, it's awesome, it's gold. AI is a bubble, or it's about kicking you out of your job. AI can't even read a clock. Stanford's report says that the top models keep getting better. But people are adopting AI faster than they picked up the personal computer or the internet. The benchmarks for measuring AI, the policies meant to govern it, and the job market are struggling to keep up. AI is sprinting, and the rest of us are trying to find our shoes.

And get this: AI companies are generating revenue way faster than companies in any previous technology boom. The difference now is that they're spending obscene amounts of cash, in the hundreds of billions of dollars, to create data centers, chips, and expand electricity.

This is my attempt to make sense of that transformation — to trace the arc from the birth of the universe to the birth of artificial intelligence, from the first flicker of consciousness to the coming age of synthetic minds. It is a journey through physics, biology, evolution, data, and the strange, unpredictable creature called humanity.

Every life has a turning point — a moment when the past, the present, and the future align with startling clarity. For some, it comes early. For others, it never comes at all.

For me, it arrived late, after decades of struggle, reinvention, collapse, and awakening. It arrived when I realized that everything I had lived through — the pain, the failures, the improbable means to overcome them, wandering into scientific obsessions to understand the meaning of life, the relentless curiosity that refused to die — had been preparing me for this moment in history.

A moment when humanity stands at the edge of a transformation so vast, so irreversible, and so consequential that most people cannot yet see it.

A moment when the concept of time itself detaches from its seemingly linear flow, when the meaning of intelligence itself becomes questionable, and when the future appears to be accelerating towards us faster than our mammalian minds can comprehend. We are entering a discontinuity, a time when the world will likely become a different place from the one we are accustomed to, and it will need clarity and a deeper understanding across all facets of life, free of distractions. Wisdom, not noise, and comfort, not hype, will be needed to deal with the fast-approaching future.

I am still not qualified to pursue any of these subjects academically, for I am far removed from ever being able to revisit the rigors of learning required to grasp the basics of a subject with a steep learning curve. It has already been an enormous solo learning undertaking, with no guidance on systematically structuring my progression from one subject to the next. Going back to college to attend classes while running a business was an option, reminiscent of how I earned my Master's degree back in 1987 while working full-time. But I knew this time around, in the middle of responsibilities, raising a family, I wouldn't be able to repeat that feat. I had fallen on hard times during those years, so that was never an option for several personal reasons. But with a gnawing inner urge to learn about AI, I acquired another trait, an instinct to understand science from a non-academic viewpoint based purely on experience. Curiosity is a wonderful thing; it keeps the brain from rotting. Learning new things outside your domain of experience is one solid way I personally put curiosity to work.

Most of us over the age of 50 don't realize that when asked a question outside our understanding, we usually dump responses without thinking — from some corner of our brain. Those responses always come from cached memory. Identical opinions you formed decades ago. Same mental models. Same reactions to new information. In AI terms, you're a frozen model: no longer training, just running inference on outdated weights.

What is a Singularity

From Apple, American Scientific dated today:

Albert Einstein's general theory of relativity tells us that the universe's backdrop is a fluid continuum, space-time, in which neither space nor time has an absolute meaning. What's more, at the Big Bang, space-time distorts into a point of infinite density called a singularity. We can't say this is where time begins, only that it marks a rupture beyond which we cannot extrapolate. This situation is also termed the singularity, which denotes a time beyond which a new paradigm for biological beings emerges.

Various solutions to the field equations of Albert Einstein's general theory of relativity describe an infinite distortion of space and time known as a black hole. The heart of a black hole is a singularity — a point with zero volume and infinite mass.

Gravitational singularities are points in space-time where gravitational forces compress matter to an infinite density, resulting in an object with no volume and infinite mass, commonly associated with black holes. These singularities emerge from the collapse of massive stars when they exhaust their nuclear fuel.

According to general relativity, every black hole has a singularity inside. For a non-rotating black hole, this region is a single point; for a rotating black hole, it is smeared into a ring singularity lying in the plane of rotation. In both cases, the singular region has zero volume.

Singularity in time, not in space, is a region where temperatures and densities go to unlimited numbers — you can't go past a certain point. As x gets smaller, 1/x gets bigger and tends to infinity. This kind of Singularity is a neighborhood, and hovers around that spot because x can never become 0 while tending towards infinity. And the neighborhood where time stops, or time = 0, is also not a possibility, because physics demands motion. So how did our world come into being? That's a different problem for physicists to figure out, but for anyone interested in learning more, I will attempt an answer later, once I complete my book, with references to further reading.

The Technological Singularity I am writing about here is the point in human evolution when the pace of machine intelligence surpasses the collective intelligence of all human beings who have ever lived. Once we cross that point, we cannot predict what will happen after that. This is the point in the curve where everything that was moving linearly suddenly curves upwards and becomes vertical. We are currently on the knee curve of this turn, very close to going vertical with the machines.

The technological singularity is not a prediction; it is a trajectory along a curve indicating that human evolution is about to cross a threshold beyond which nothing can be predicted. A moment when recursive self‑improvement, compute scaling, and global competition collide with the limits of human adaptability. The San Francisco Consensus — a loose network of researchers, founders, and investors — places the arrival of Artificial General Intelligence around 2028, and Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) by the end of 2030. There are, of course, disagreements about these dates, but they are not about whether; it is about how soon.

This manuscript is written with urgency because the world is changing faster than the collective human intellect can keep pace, and our institutions, laws, and imaginations are failing to keep up. Throughout this document, my main focus is on the coming technological singularity, scheduled for sometime in 2029. But to start writing about the subject without the backdrop of life and how we got here would be a disconnect. Note that this has the word 'technological' before 'singularity'. It's different from the singularity commonly referred to for the Big Bang, although there are raging debates amongst physicists against using that term for the universe's beginning. You might wonder if I am even qualified to write about such a technical subject, and you will be right, but that would rob me and you of the opportunity to learn what is possible when all you see is darkness. I found a lot of light over time, and I wanted to share it with you. It covers an important subject written in prose that you will understand, and the urgency it beckons.

I sincerely hope this compendium provides a fresh viewpoint on life, not from the mind of a physicist, but from one that you might relate to more readily. This book exists because I want to share the new ways of looking at life I learned on my own and found deeper meaning. I want to remove the confusion we all have, yet we never get clear answers when we need them. I no longer want to stay silent.

It is also, unapologetically, a personal journey. I mix anecdotes and a touch of humor in my writing because the world we live in can use a dose of humor amid its absurdity. I write with the zeal of a child loaded with curiosity, because curiosity is the only antidote to fear.

Suppose you feel a little disoriented as you read, good. That means you're paying attention, and my convictions are coming through. Suppose you feel a little exhilarated, even better. That means you're alive at exactly the right moment in history.

Buckle up — we're diving straight into the first part of the book, where I begin with our universe having the obvious stage. This section creates a visible arc for us to follow: from evolution and consciousness to Artificial Intelligence, and the unfathomable risks we are hurtling towards.

I have tried to keep my voice analytical, curious, and unafraid to poke the universe with a stick.

Welcome to learning about the singularity we are in. Let's begin.
